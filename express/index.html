01

The indestructible practice

Vajra means the thunderbolt and the diamond at once — the thing that cannot be broken. That's the practice we teach: a body that's strong enough to be still.

We're a small studio. Classes cap at sixteen, so a teacher can actually see you, adjust you, and call you by name. We lean into the warrior poses — grounded, deliberate, demanding — and pair them with the breathwork that makes them sustainable.

You don't need to be flexible, fit, or experienced. You need to show up. The rest is built on the mat.

02

Three warriors, one line of force

Virabhadrasana I, II, and III are a sequence, not three separate poses. Each builds on the last — rooted, then open, then balanced on a single leg.

01

The Riser

Virabhadrasana I

Hips square to the front, back heel planted, arms rising overhead. The foundation pose — power drawn straight up from the ground.

02

The Gaze

Virabhadrasana II

Chest open, arms reaching front and back, front knee tracking over the ankle. Eyes settle past the front fingertips — steady, unhurried.

03

The Balance

Virabhadrasana III

Hinge forward onto one standing leg until body and lifted leg form a single straight line, parallel to the floor. Stillness becomes the whole pose.
